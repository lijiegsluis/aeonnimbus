# Fabrinet (FN): The Foundry the Market Priced Like a Supplier

**Aeon Nimbus Research** · Equity Deep Dive · Fifteenth position · Long · NYSE: FN
Published October 2, 2026 · Prices as of October 2 close · Analyst: LiJie Guo

Fabrinet grew revenue 36% to \$4.64 billion in fiscal 2026 while its former largest customer, Nvidia, fell about 20%. Strip Nvidia out and the rest of the business grew 57%. Data center is now 51% of the quarterly mix, and Q1 FY27 is guided up 43%.

The stock fell 20% the day after that print and closed October 2 at \$463.69, 38% below its May high, at 32.9× trailing EPS against 35.2× at its 52-week low a year ago, when growth was half today's rate. We believe the market is pricing one customer's share loss as a verdict on the whole foundry. Our \$580 target needs no re-rating: it is 27.5× our FY28E economic EPS of \$21.11, against 27.7× today on FY27E. Our own DCF (\$276) and house blend (\$394) both sit below the price, and we say so.

## Macro: rates and the discount rate

The US 10-year yield closed at about 5.25% on October 2 (5.252% per CNBC; another end-of-day print shows 5.29%), after touching 5.34% on October 1, its highest since 2002, and against about 4.7% on August 18. The September payrolls report that morning showed +29,000 jobs against about 85,000 expected and unemployment at 4.2%; the ten-year fell to 5.18% on the print and then reversed, and fed funds futures now price roughly 77% to 84% odds that the Fed holds in October. On September 16 the Fed raised its target range 25bp to 3.75% to 4.00% in a unanimous 12-0 vote, its first hike since 2023 (FOMC statement). Sixteen of 19 participants expect at least one more this year. Chairman Warsh named competition for debt from the AI build-out as one reason Treasury yields have risen. Brent crude was above \$100 in September in our macro file, and FN's own 10-K names the US-Iran war among geopolitical risks. This regime affects the thesis directly. At a 5.25% risk-free rate our cost of equity for FN is 11.9%, and at that rate a ten-year DCF values the business at \$276, below the stock. FN has no borrowings on its balance sheet (a \$75M term loan was signed after year-end), so it has no refinancing exposure to speak of, but its equity duration is long: 64% of our DCF value sits in the terminal year. A 50bp move in the ten-year moves intrinsic value by roughly \$20 a share. The position is a call that earnings delivery outruns the rate headwind over twelve months, and we size it as such.

## Business model

Fabrinet does not design optical products. Cisco, Nvidia, Nokia and Amazon design them; Fabrinet builds them. Its product is manufacturing capability: precision optical packaging, fibre alignment, hermetic sealing, burn-in and test, run at volume across three campuses in Thailand, with a new-product-introduction site in Santa Clara and a customised-optics plant in Fuzhou, China. The company employs 21,521 people, 21,020 of them in manufacturing, in more than four million square feet. Customers pay a per-unit price that passes most of the bill of materials through, so gross margin is 12.2% and says little on its own.

The economics sit below the gross line. Customers carry the R&D and Fabrinet carries almost no selling effort, so SG&A was 2.0% of revenue in FY26 (1.3% on a non-GAAP basis in Q4) and the non-GAAP operating margin reached 10.9% in Q4, a three-year high. Three features make the position hard to dislodge. Qualification takes three to six months or longer on each product. Fabrinet runs a "factory within a factory" with segregated teams so each customer's intellectual property stays walled off. And it does not sell modules of its own, so it competes with none of the customers it serves; the 10-K calls this the point of difference against every rival that does.

#### Fabrinet (foundry)

Builds customer designs. No own-brand optics. GM ~12%, SG&A about 2% of revenue, operating margin ~11%. Growth gated by qualified floor space, not by R&D.

#### Module and component makers

Own lasers, chips and modules (Innolight, Eoptolink, Lumentum, Coherent). Gross margins of 40% to 46%. Win share on cost and speed, and carry the pricing and inventory risk.

#### General EMS (Celestica, Jabil)

Build servers, switches and racks. Operating margins 6% to 8%. Broad, with less optical process depth.

The limits are as clear. Gross margin is 12%, so a small pricing or yield error moves earnings. Four customers are 57.4% of revenue. Free cash flow was \$4 million in FY26. Capital intensity has stepped up, with capex at 5.4% of revenue against 3.5% a year earlier. The chairman and CEO roles are combined and insiders hold 0.24%. Production is concentrated in one Thai province. FN deserves a premium to a generic contract manufacturer for its returns, its execution record and its place in the bottleneck, and no premium for margin or customer diversification. It trades at a discount to Celestica, which earns a lower margin but grows faster. The thesis therefore rests on earnings growth and needs no re-rating.

## Industry and competition

Independent research from Cignal AI puts worldwide datacom optical component revenue at \$7.7 billion in the first calendar quarter of 2026, double the year before. Chinese-headquartered suppliers account for roughly 60% of it. Innolight alone booked \$2.6 billion, a 34% share up from 25%, which is about 2.4 times Eoptolink at \$1.1 billion; Innolight, Eoptolink and HG Genuine together hold 51%. Counterpoint estimates that Innolight and Eoptolink supply about 60% of Nvidia's 800G volume. Fabrinet has been losing that share at its former largest customer, and the market has treated the Nvidia line as the whole thesis.

Our reading differs in one respect. Module makers capture the value (Innolight's gross margin was reported at 46% in Q1 2026) and also carry the pricing, inventory and technology risk. A foundry takes a thin gross margin on a customer's design and no pricing risk on the module. Cignal AI notes that assembly, test and qualified capacity, not lasers, is the binding constraint at the high end, and that "it would take years" before Fabrinet could ramp enough to replace Chinese volume. The sector is short of the one thing Fabrinet sells. Management said on the Q3 call that the constraint is supply, not demand, naming lasers, memory and certain ASICs as the items holding shipments below orders.

### FCC rule status

Reuters reported on August 4 that the FCC was drafting a ban on new Chinese optical transceivers, and Innolight was added to the Pentagon's 1260H list on June 8. The FCC's Third Report and Order (FCC 26-50, adopted July 22, published in the Federal Register on September 11 at 91 FR 57798) takes effect on October 13, 2026. It bars equipment authorization for devices that contain a logic-bearing hardware component produced by an entity on the FCC's Covered List, and applies to new applications only. Commenters cited optical transceivers among the examples, but the order itself names no transceiver maker and does not list Innolight or Eoptolink; it bites only if they are added to the Covered List, which has not happened. A further notice in the same order proposes broader measures: any component, bills of materials and term limits on authorizations. Cignal AI said before the order that it did not expect a ban to stick, and that contract manufacturers such as Fabrinet, Sanmina and Jabil would benefit only with large capacity investment and confidence that rules would not reverse within months.

We have therefore removed a Chinese-transceiver restriction from our base and bull cases. It remains a tail upside: roughly half of the world's module capacity would be in scope of a broader rule, and Fabrinet is the largest independent Western-aligned option. It is also a tail risk, because retaliation would hit indium phosphide supply on which every Western laser maker depends.

## Capital allocation and governance

Fabrinet repurchased \$125.7 million of stock in FY25 and \$5.2 million in FY26, and made none in Q4. The cash went into capacity instead. A \$169 million authorisation remains. The balance sheet funds the plan: \$875 million of cash and investments with no borrowings at year-end, trade payables of \$1.0 billion that finance most of the inventory, and a \$75 million term loan with a Thai bank that the CFO disclosed in August. The CFO guides FY27 capex near \$250 million, flat on FY26. The 10-K does flag that additional capital could dilute holders; our invalidation list treats any equity raise as thesis-breaking.

Seamus Grady is both chairman and chief executive officer, Csaba Sverha is chief financial officer, and directors and executive officers own 0.24% of the shares (2025 proxy). Thin alignment for a company asking investors to underwrite \$250 million or more a year of capex. Share-based compensation (\$34.6 million) is 0.75% of revenue and is charged in our DCF. The Cayman Islands incorporation brings the usual limits on shareholder remedies and a 10-K risk factor about passive foreign investment company status for US holders. The Amazon warrant is described above. We rate governance adequate and not a reason to own or avoid the stock.

## Tax and currency

Pillar Two is now a recurring cost. For FY26 Fabrinet booked \$57.4 million of current tax under Thailand's enacted undertaxed profits rule, all recognised in Q4 and held in income tax payable; cash taxes paid in the year were \$29.4 million. Fabrinet's home jurisdiction has not adopted the rules, and Thailand taxes up the low-taxed income of other group entities. Management's non-GAAP EPS excludes the charge from Q4 FY26 on, and the Q1 FY27 guide strips \$0.40 a share. At that rate the annual cost is about \$1.60 a share, or 10% of non-GAAP EPS. We assume it scales with profit, so FY27E economic EPS sits \$2.07 below non-GAAP. Thailand may introduce investment-support measures that offset part of it; none is confirmed.

Currency matters because contracts bill in dollars while most payroll is in Thai baht. The 10-K reports that the dollar has fallen 9.4% against the baht since June 2024, and Q2 FY26 carried a \$3.2 million foreign exchange loss, worth \$0.09 a share. Hedges run up to 12 months. A further 5% baht appreciation would not change our thesis; we estimate it would cost a few tenths of a point of margin (the company discloses no sensitivity), and we flag it as a risk. The 10-K also warns that the Thai government could repeal promotional certificates and tax holidays that Fabrinet enjoys.

## Target price derivation

A 12-month target is the price at which the stock should trade at the end of the horizon, on the information then available. At the end of September 2027 the market will be valuing FY28 (July 2027 to June 2028) as the current fiscal year, exactly as it values FY27 today. That lets us compare like with like: today's price over FY27E earnings, against the target price over FY28E earnings.

| Step                           | Calculation                                                                                            | Result  |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------|
| 1\. Today's multiple           | \$463.69 divided by FY27E economic EPS of \$16.75                                                      | 27.7×   |
| 2\. FY28E economic EPS         | Revenue \$7,895M (+26%), 11.2% non-GAAP operating margin, \$25M interest income, 15% tax, 36.6M shares | \$21.11 |
| 3\. Hold the multiple constant | \$21.11 × 27.7                                                                                         | \$585   |
| 4\. Adjust the multiple        | 27.5× is 0.22 turns below 27.7×, worth -0.8%                                                           | \$580   |
| Return from today's price      | EPS growth +26.1%, multiple -0.8%                                                                      | +25.1%  |

All of the move from today to the target is earnings growth; the multiple is a small drag. The \$580 target sits \$5 below the constant-multiple value (\$585), so it does not depend on the multiple holding. Three checks on the multiple follow. First, at \$580 FN would trade at 34.6× trailing economic EPS (FY27E) against 35.2× at its October 2025 low, when growth was half as fast: the stock would be priced at no higher a trailing multiple than at its trough. Second, 27.5× is 25% below the 36.5× midpoint of the two peer groups. Third, it is 20% below Celestica's 34.3×, which has a lower margin but faster growth. Against those, FY28 growth of 26% is lower than FY27's 35%, and a slowing grower usually earns a lower multiple, so we assume no expansion.

## Investment thesis

We make two claims and keep them apart. We do not claim FN is cheap on cash flows: our DCF is \$276 against a price of \$463.69, and the market would need a 9.0% discount rate to justify it. We do claim a tactical mispricing: the stock fell by two-fifths while its earnings rose, the reaction to the August print was twice the options-implied move on a beat-and-raise, and most of the concerns behind the sale are already in the numbers or are reversing.

The whole decline is multiple compression. Part of it is sector-wide and part is rates. ### Where the evidence is weaker

FN is not cheap against its own long history. At 35.5× trailing GAAP earnings (34.6× on October 1) it sits above its five-year median of 26.0× (GuruFocus), a period in which growth averaged half today's rate. It is priced at a discount to Celestica (27.8× against 34.3× run-rate). The Street's average target (\$734.11 across nine analysts) is above ours, so we are not the bull in the room. And FN's customer base and Thai concentration are risks the multiple has to carry. Our case is narrower than "undervalued": it is that a 35% grower with a 26% to 31% return on capital, a pre-announced step-up in capacity and an industry short of its product is priced for a deceleration the evidence does not yet show, and that earnings the company is on course to deliver, at no higher a multiple than today's, are worth about \$580.

### Thesis pillars

Pillar one · Diversification

### Diversification: ex-Nvidia revenue grew 57%

The 10-K puts Nvidia at 27.6% of FY25 revenue and 16.3% of FY26, roughly \$944 million falling to \$757 million, a 20% decline (B. Riley estimated 21%). Ex-Nvidia, revenue went from \$2.48 billion to \$3.88 billion. Cisco grew 48% to \$924 million. Amazon crossed 10% through the high-performance computing program and is now a hyperscaler-direct transceiver customer, ramping in the September quarter. A second merchant transceiver customer starts production in the December quarter, initially in 800G short-reach with 1.6T later. Optical circuit switching, low-Earth-orbit satellites, lidar, EV charging and industrial lasers are all described by management as emerging contributors. A further 30% fall in Nvidia revenue would remove \$227 million, 4.9% of FY26 revenue and about one-seventh of the revenue growth in our FY27 estimate.

Pillar two · Capacity converts at low overhead

### Capacity converts to profit at about 11 cents per revenue dollar

Incremental revenue reaches operating profit at about 11%. Every additional \$1 billion of revenue adds about \$110 million of operating profit, or \$2.57 of economic EPS. Capacity is \$5.8 billion now and about \$9.8 billion by the March quarter with Building 10, Navanakorn and Santa Clara; our FY28E revenue of \$7.9 billion uses 81% of it. FY29E revenue of \$9.5 billion would need 97% of it, so Buildings 11 and 12 (about \$2.1 billion each, not announced) are implicit in our out-years and in our FY29 capex of 4.5% of revenue.

The CFO guides FY27 capex near \$250 million, funded from cash flow and the balance sheet, with a recently signed \$75 million Thai term loan. We model FY27 unlevered free cash flow of about \$67 million, because working capital at a constant 72-day cycle absorbs about \$325 million. The position depends on Building 10 filling on schedule and the cycle not lengthening.

Pillar three · The derating outran the fundamentals

### Valuation: below 52-week-low multiples with twice the growth

On October 7, 2025, at the 52-week low of \$358.28, FN traded at 3.5× trailing sales and 35.2× trailing non-GAAP EPS while growing 19%. Today it trades at 3.4× and 32.9× while guiding 43%. At the May 14 high of \$748.89 the same measures were 6.2× and 59.2×. FN fell 20.3% on August 18 with Coherent and AAOI down double digits the same day, and 6% on September 14 when Corning filed its \$2 billion program. Neither event changed FN's order book. The counter-argument is the five-year median above, and a discount rate that has risen and not yet reversed.

## Institutional case

Fabrinet is the largest independent optical foundry: it builds transceivers, coherent modules, data center interconnect systems and HPC optics designed by Cisco, Nvidia, Nokia and Amazon, in Thailand, at a 12% gross margin, about 1.3% non-GAAP SG&A and an 11% operating margin, with no borrowings at year-end and \$875M of cash. FY26 revenue grew 36% to \$4.64B and Q1 FY27 is guided up 43%; ex-Nvidia revenue grew 57% while Nvidia fell 20%. The stock is down 38% from May at \$464, 28× run-rate EPS against Celestica at 34× despite a higher operating margin, and below its own 52-week-low multiples on both P/E and EV/Sales. Capacity steps from \$5.8B today to about \$9.8B by the March quarter with Building 10, Navanakorn and Santa Clara. We are long from \$405.33 (Sep 28) with a \$580 target (27.5× FY28E economic EPS of \$21.11, about 0.2 turn below today's forward multiple), a \$337 stop (5.9% below the 52-week low) and a 5% size. Intrinsic DCF at an 11.9% WACC is \$276 and the house blend is \$394; the target is the median of our scenario distribution (mean \$557.50) and an earnings-delivery call.

## Definitions

Economic EPS  
Non-GAAP EPS (which excludes share-based compensation) with the Thai Pillar Two top-up tax charged at a 15% overall rate. Management's non-GAAP figure leaves the tax out.

Pillar Two (UTPR)  
The OECD's 15% global minimum tax. Thailand has enacted an undertaxed profits rule that taxes low-taxed income of other entities in the group.

DCI  
Data center interconnect: coherent optical systems that link data centers over distance.

Foundry  
In this report, a contract manufacturer that builds products to a customer's design and does not sell its own branded product.

Run-rate  
The latest guided quarter multiplied by four.

Half-Kelly  
Half of the position size that maximises long-run growth given the win probability and payoff ratio; we hold well below it.
