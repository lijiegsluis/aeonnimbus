# Marketing Psychology

## Procedure
1. Identify the asset, audience, and the single action wanted.
2. Audit it against the principles below; list which are present, missing, or misused.
3. Propose concrete rewrites (before/after), each tagged with the principle used.
4. Rank by expected impact and effort; suggest an A/B test for the top 2.

## Principles (non-exhaustive, pick the few that fit)
- **Pricing**: anchoring, decoy effect, charm pricing, rounded vs precise price, price framing per day, bundling, tiered good/better/best, free-trial endowment, pain of paying.
- **Choice**: loss aversion, scarcity (only if real), paradox of choice, defaults, commitment/consistency, foot-in-the-door.
- **Trust**: social proof, authority, liking, reciprocity, specificity, costly signals, risk reversal.
- **Attention**: curiosity gap, Von Restorff (stand-out), serial position, peak-end, framing, concreteness.
- **Motivation**: goal gradient, endowment, sunk cost, IKEA effect, identity, fresh-start effect.

## Rules
- Persuade honestly: no fake scarcity, fake testimonials, or hidden costs. Regulated products (investment research) must keep required disclaimers and avoid performance promises.
- Always say what the principle does and why it fits this audience.
